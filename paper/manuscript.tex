\begin{abstract}
Grimmett and Holmes ask whether almost every finite Markov kernel coalesces under every consistent grand coupling. We answer yes. A coupling of rank $k\ge2$ has a minimum-image idempotent. Averaging its permutation action on that image imposes $k-1$ independent affine conditions on selected rows of the marginal kernel. A finite union of these affine subspaces contains every exceptional kernel. Off that union, each fixed kernel also admits an exponential tail bound uniform over its grand couplings.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{The grand coupling and the marginal kernel}
Let $S=[n]$ and let $P$ be a row-stochastic matrix. A grand coupling consistent with $P$ is a law $\mu$ on maps $f:S\to S$ with $\mu\{f:f(i)=j\}=P_{ij}$. Independent, identically distributed maps $F_1,F_2,\ldots$ of law $\mu$ act successively on all initial states. Write $k(\mu)$ for the eventual size of the image of $S$, and $K(P)$ for the set of possible $k(\mu)$ over couplings. Full coalescence is the case $k(\mu)=1$.

Open Problem~3.8 of Grimmett and Holmes \cite[p.~8]{grimhol} asks whether $K(P)=\{1\}$ almost surely when the $n^2$ entries before row normalization are independent uniform variables on $(0,1)$. Their Theorem~3.5 and Remark~3.7 give a sufficient stationary-mass condition holding with positive probability. Theorem~4.3 gives a doubly stochastic lumped kernel for block measures \cite{grimhol,ghprior}. Theorem~4.4 connects them to deterministic coalescing classes, whereas Examples~3.10 and~4.5 show that classes can be random. We use one representative row per idempotent fiber, allowing arbitrary coupling supports.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:affine}
If some consistent grand coupling has eventual rank $k\ge2$, then $P$ lies in an explicit finite union of affine subspaces of codimension $k-1$ in the row-sum-one affine space. Hence for almost every kernel under any absolutely continuous law on that space, every grand coupling coalesces almost surely. In particular this holds for the normalized uniform kernel in Open Problem~3.8.
\end{theorem}

\section{Minimum rank and the row equations}
The finite semigroup generated by $\supp\mu$ consists of the nonempty compositions of its maps. Let $k$ be its minimum rank, meaning the smallest image size. A word giving rank $k$ recurs almost surely among disjoint independent blocks, since each required map has positive probability. Applying that word to any current image leaves at most $k$ states. Every nonempty composition has at least $k$ states in its image, and image size never increases. The eventual rank is therefore $k$ almost surely.

Fix a minimum-rank map $g$ and write $C=g(S)$. Its restriction to $C$ is a permutation: otherwise $g^2$ would have image smaller than $k$. Taking a positive power of $g$ gives an idempotent $e$ with image $C$ and identity restriction to $C$. For each $f\in\supp\mu$, the image of $e\circ f\circ e$ lies in $C$ and has size at least $k$. Thus $e\circ f$ permutes $C$. This is elementary minimum-ideal structure, related to the local groups of H\"ogn\"as \cite[Theorem~2]{hognas}.

Let $B_j=e^{-1}(j)$ for each $j\in C$. The averaged permutation matrix on $C$ has entries
\[
               A_{ij}=\sum_{x\in B_j}P_{ix},\qquad i,j\in C .
\]
In particular every column of $A$ sums to one. These are the claimed equations: fix an idempotent $e$ and impose $\sum_{i\in C}\sum_{x\in B_j}P_{ix}=1$ for all $j\in C$. Their sum is automatic. Vary one selected row by transfers among the $k$ representative columns. This realizes every zero-sum change in the $k$ grouped column sums, so there are exactly $k-1$ independent constraints. The affine subspace is nonempty, for example it contains the identity kernel.

There are finitely many idempotents. For every rank $k\ge2$ the associated subspaces have measure zero relative to the row-sum-one affine space. Their union covers every kernel admitting even one noncoalescing law. The normalization in the source has a density in these coordinates: for one row write $q_i=s p_i$ with $s=\sum_i q_i$ and $\sum_i p_i=1$. In coordinates $s,p_1,\ldots,p_{n-1}$ the absolute Jacobian is $s^{n-1}$, then integrating out $s$ gives a density on the simplex. The rows are independent. Every stochastic kernel has at least one consistent coupling, obtained by choosing the images of individual states independently according to the rows. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:affine}. The case $n=1$ coalesces at time zero.

\section{A uniform tail for a fixed certified kernel}
Assume $n\ge2$ and that $P$ avoids all the displayed subspaces of rank at least two. For this $P$ write
\[
 \Delta=\min_{\substack{e^2=e\\|\operatorname{im}e|\ge2}}
   \ \max_{j\in \operatorname{im}e}
       \left|\sum_{i\in \operatorname{im}e}\sum_{x:e(x)=j}P_{ix}-1\right|>0.
\]
Put $M=n^n$ and $\eta=\min\{1/(2M),\,\Delta/(2nM)\}$. Given any consistent law, retain maps of probability at least $\eta$. The discarded mass is at most $M\eta\le1/2$. If the retained maps generate no constant map, their semigroup has a minimum-rank idempotent $e$ of rank at least two. In the corresponding compression a retained map contributes exactly one to each column count. A discarded map contributes between zero and $n$. Consequently the full average differs from one by at most $nM\eta$ in each column. This would give $\Delta\le nM\eta\le\Delta/2$. The retained maps therefore have a synchronizing word.

A shortest synchronizing word cannot repeat a nonsingleton image along its successive prefixes: deleting the repeated portion shortens it. Its length is at most $L=2^n-2$, which bounds the number of nonsingleton subsets encountered. Every independent block of $L$ draws has probability at least $\eta^L$ to begin with that word. Thus for the coalescence time $\tau$ and all integers $b\ge0$,
\[
                 \Pr_{\mu}(\tau>bL)\le(1-\eta^L)^b.
\]
All constants here depend on the fixed kernel and $n$, and are uniform in $\mu$. The affine obstruction gives a sufficient certificate on its complement. The argument does not characterize the kernels lying on the subspaces.

\section{A three-state example}
Take $e=(1,2,1)$ and $f=(2,1,1)$, using tuples of images. Both maps preserve $C=\{1,2\}$ and induce, respectively, identity and swap. A law giving both maps positive mass has eventual rank two. Which pair of initial states first coalesces depends on the first draw: $\{1,3\}$ under $e$ and $\{2,3\}$ under $f$. The fibers of the particular idempotent $e$ are $\{1,3\}$ and $\{2\}$. With equal weights the compression from the representative rows is a two-by-two matrix with every entry $1/2$.

Now give the constant map $(1,1,1)$ mass $\varepsilon$ and each previous map mass $(1-\varepsilon)/2$. The compressed column sums for $e$ become $1+\varepsilon$ and $1-\varepsilon$. For $\varepsilon>0$, the pictured support coalesces when the constant map arrives. For this family the whole affine certificate can also be checked directly. For $C=\{1,2\}$ both rank-two idempotents have grouped column sums $1+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon$, since column three is zero. For $C=\{1,3\}$ the column total for the fiber containing $3$ is either zero or $(1-\varepsilon)/2$. For $C=\{2,3\}$ it is either zero or $1+(1+\varepsilon)/2$. The rank-three identity has column three zero. Thus $\varepsilon>0$ takes the kernel off all seven subspaces in this example, and every grand coupling of it coalesces.
